    214 DAYS ON THE CLOCK · THE COMPACT EXPIRES MIDNIGHT, DEC 31, 2084

    An original continuity of the Cyberpunk universe — unofficial fan work

    01 · Logline

    Days after an orbital nuclear catastrophe, a seventeen-year-old full-spectrum Resonator escapes Bloodline, carrying the partial consciousnesses of two dead boys. As Domingo City moves toward independence and Bloodline’s emergency charter runs out, every government, corporation, religion, movement, and family tries to decide what the three boys are for.

    RESONATOR — implants that turn a person’s own biology into elemental-scale power.

    BLOODLINE — the corporation that builds them.

    DOMINGO CITY — Gulf metropolis, sovereign Republic of Texas.

    Notes for the room

    This is the world bible’s logline, near-verbatim. The emergency charter is the Matagorda Compact — the ten-year deal that gave Bloodline extraordinary power over the city; it is the clock on the cover. Everything in the deck hangs off this one sentence: a fugitive, a countdown, and a city-sized custody dispute.

    02 · The Hook

    Three boys share one body. Two of them are dead.

    Their city has 214 days to decide who owns its future.

    Every power on the Gulf wants the same seventeen-year-old — each for a different reason.

    Notes for the room

    If this slide lands, the rest of the deck is confirmation. The three sentences map to the deck’s three acts: the body (slides 8–13), the deadline (slides 14–15), and the claims (slide 14’s six-way pull). Nothing here is a metaphor — all three statements are literally true in the story.

    03 · The Continuity

    One trunk. One clean break.

    Chromopunk inherits Cyberpunk’s foundation whole — Night City, the Corporate Wars, the DataKrash. In 2045 the branch becomes permanent: space can be owned, and chrome becomes living tissue. Same wounds, different future. Nothing official is overwritten — it is inherited, then left behind.

    Notes for the room

    The divergence has three engines, all pre-1970 in origin: the Outer Space Treaty never enters force, so settlement creates ownership; the Cold War becomes four-sided and permanent; and redesigning workers’ bodies becomes cheaper than making environments safe. Familiar disasters still happen — different causes, same wounds — until 2045, when mature space territories and living chrome push history onto its own track.

    04 · The Thesis

    “Does creating, protecting, financing, or depending upon something grant the right to own it?”

    NATIONSask it about the heavens.
    TEXASasks it about Domingo City.
    DOMINGOasks it about the territory beneath it.
    BLOODLINEasks it about Resonators.
    VIN— the seventeen-year-old — asks it about his own body.

    Every empire in the setting answers yes. The story tests the answer.

    Notes for the room

    This is the deck’s thesis and the bible’s stated governing question, verbatim. Every conflict in the book — territorial, corporate, familial, and the one inside Vin’s skull — is this question at a different scale. It is why the pitch works for publishing and for a universe: one question, infinitely fractal.

    THE COMPACT — BLOODLINE’S EMERGENCY CHARTER · EXPIRES DEC 31, 2084

    Notes for the room

    By 2084, roughly twenty-three million people live off Earth, and five off-world wars have already been fought over routes, water, and inherited claims. The master timeline (1967–2084) is fully built in the world bible: eras, wars, treaties, and the corporate collapse chain that ends with three corporations holding pieces of one orbital deterrent.

    06 · The Setting

    Not Night City with a Gulf accent

    Domingo City: a crescent metropolis of 8.6 million wrapped around Matagorda Bay, inside the sovereign Republic of Texas. Caribbean, Latin American, Black Texan, Tejano, maritime, hurricane, and labor identities — a founder’s corporate project that its workers turned into their own city. Now it wants out.

    LIBERTY — the port engine
    DOMINGO CENTER — the corporate mask
    SUNTOWN — the technology corridor
    DORADO — the chrome district
    LITTLE ANTONIO — the capital
    LINEWOOD — where the city stores consequences
    + SOUTH COLUMBIA — the unrecognized territory that refuses to be a district at all
    Notes for the room

    Founded 2001 under a Texan development charter; built by Dominican, Puerto Rican, Tejano, Black Texan, Mexican, Vietnamese, Filipino, and Caribbean labor. The city’s three geometries — the Bay, the Walls, the Lines — sort wealth by elevation and transit. South Columbia mirrors the whole plot: Domingo demands independence from Texas while claiming a coast that refuses to be claimed.

    07 · The System

    Resonance is not magic

    Bloodline’s BEA-V7 system activates a dormant genetic trait and shapes it through living chrome — cybernetics grown into tissue. The result: seven spectra of elemental-like force. Every effect has a biological source, a mechanism, an environmental requirement, and a physical cost. No exceptions. That discipline is the pitch.

    FIRE — stored heat WATER — moves existing liquid EARTH — vibration & magnetism WIND — pressure & flow ICE — moves heat away LIGHTNING — stored charge BLOOD — circulation itself
    Notes for the room

    Water needs liquid; Wind needs atmosphere; Fire cooks its own user. Output classes run from utility work to apotheotic prototypes, and one in 1,800 people carries the trait — about 72,000 activated Resonators exist worldwide. Confluence — running more than one spectrum in one nervous system — is nearly impossible. Vin runs all seven. That is not a power fantasy; it is a medical emergency with a name.

    08 · The Dependency Engine

    Power increases dependency

    Activation starts a progressive degeneration — the Rot. One drug manages it: Reprieve, Bloodline’s dependency medicine, dosed every seventy-two hours through Bloodline-authenticated hardware. The harder a Resonator burns, the faster the Rot advances. Medicine becomes leash, payroll, border, and sentence.

    The world’s cruelest business model — and its central metaphor.

    ACTIVATE→ EMPLOY→ DEPEND→ DECLINE→ CUSTODY
    Notes for the room

    A standard regimen: an injection every seventy-two hours, daily regulators, monthly tuning — priced so that resignation is medically dangerous. No division of Bloodline planned this as a conspiracy; every division simply profits when the patient stays inside. The bible calls it the Blood Loop: identify, recruit, activate, employ, indebt, stabilize, reclassify, extract.

    CHOICE

    CBE — Confluence Bioelectric Enhancement: Bloodline’s classified program to run three boys as one system.

    Before every test they said each other’s names aloud — “Elik Baptiste. Tomas Vega. Vicente Rodríguez.” Names before numbers.

    Notes for the room

    The rituals are the emotional proof the program failed: the Third Knock (three knocks means one of us, not staff — after Elik dies, Vin hears three knocks inside his skull), contraband birthday food, invented news broadcasts. Elik and Tomas now exist as continuity anchors in Vin’s body — partial, opinionated, capable of hiding things. Care, Glory, Choice: three answers to being owned.

    10 · The Protagonist

    Vicente “Vin” Rodríguez

    Dark brown skin. Gold curls — a CBE side effect, not a choice; his hair was dark before. A lean seventeen-year-old frame under disproportionately heavy black-and-green confluence hardware. The silhouette reads as a weapon. The face stays visibly young.

    His want: personhood without assigned purpose.

    BORN 18 DEC 2066 CBE-03 SEVEN SPECTRA CROSSED OUT OF CUSTODY 00:17 · JUNE 1, 2084
    Notes for the room

    Captured at twelve, days after his parents’ murder; five years of engineered consent in Bloodline custody. He loves baseball and outdated statistics, lies automatically, watches hands and exits, and steals small objects without noticing. His contradiction: he demands autonomy while craving someone else to make the unbearable decisions. His arc is learning to choose his dependencies — not to escape all of them.

    11 · The Sister

    Nicole Rodríguez

    Sixteen. Vin’s sister. High Priestess of the Serpentine — the Resonator faith and mutual-aid movement — and a public saint whose authority grew around a managed lie: she was told her brother died in 2079.

    Now he is back. Brother, institution, symbol — she can keep two.

    BORN 16 FEB 2068 LATENT CARRIER · NEVER ACTIVATED HIGH PRIESTESS, 2082
    Notes for the room

    At eleven she stayed with trapped terminal patients through a blackout, reading their names over pirate radio; the broadcast made her a public figure and the institution never let go. She speaks for Resonators while never having been activated — her mother’s sealed directive forbids it. Her arc: separating faith from obedience, and learning that protecting people doesn’t entitle her to script her brother.

    12 · The Opposition

    No cartoon villains. That is the point.

    Bloodline CEO · Vin and Nicole’s uncle · 53

    Jaylin Rodríguez

    Most dangerous when he believes he is being merciful — and he is partially correct: unmanaged Resonance really would cost lives. He loves them. That is the problem.

    The Law

    Sabine Harrow

    Never argues Vin is property — argues the implants inside him are regulated corporate infrastructure.

    The Force

    Gideon Rusk

    Security marshal. Recover the asset when practical; terminate when necessary.

    The Architect

    Dr. Lucien Kade

    Wants the three boys fused into one system. “Individuality is an evolutionary inconvenience.”

    Notes for the room

    Bloodline is not a monolith: it also contains custodians preserving evidence, clinicians pushing reform, and profiteers who need the city unable to leave. Jaylin authorized the murder of Vin’s parents — his own sister — to stop a disclosure he believed would kill thousands of patients. The book’s antagonists are all someone’s reasonable answer to a real catastrophe. That is the selling point.

    13 · The Engine

    A six-month fuse

    23:48 · MAY 31, 2084 — the story opens

    The Fourfold Fall

    Orbital nuclear strikes on Washington, Night City, Nova York, and Texas. Domingo survives — barely, and not by one corporation’s heroism.

    MIDNIGHT · DEC 31, 2084 — the story ends

    The Compact expires

    Bloodline’s emergency charter runs out as the city’s independence referendum lands. Three legal orders collide at midnight.

    One boy. Six claims:

    INFRASTRUCTURE — Bloodline EVIDENCE — Texas LEVERAGE — Domingo TESTIMONY — Free Current, the Resonator rights movement REVELATION — the Serpentine FAMILY — the mothers of the dead
    Notes for the room

    The clock is structural, not decorative: the Compact’s expiration mechanics create a jurisdictional void, and Bloodline’s proposed replacement — the Continuity Charter, permanent authority dressed as public safety — is the endgame. Vin crosses out of custody at 00:17 on June 1; everything after is these six claims closing in while the referendum builds.

    Appendix A · The Ending

    The Distributed City

    Vin, Elik, and Tomas divide CITYHEART’s authority across district utilities, public hospitals, worker networks, Resonator cooperatives, and South Columbia. No single institution can hold the city’s life again. The referendum passes: 56.2%. Independence is declared January 1, 2085. Jaylin survives to face consequence — still arguing the city will prove him right.

    The price

    Blackouts and medical failures in transition. Some Grid residents — terminal patients wired into Bloodline’s deep infrastructure — die, because the system was designed to punish removal. No clean victory: Jaylin’s warnings were not entirely false.

    Notes for the room

    Texas withdraws from intervention without recognizing separation; South Columbia gets non-annexation recognition; Linewood gains autonomy guarantees but remains unequal; Bloodline fractures into public trusts, remnants, and prizes. Elik and Tomas survive as distinct persons. The ending answers the thesis: dependence stays — ownership goes.

    Appendix B · The Mystery Ledger

    Public lie → hidden truth → cost of truth

    Why did Domingo survive?

    The lieBloodline’s flawless defense.
    The truthJaylin aimed CITYHEART’s protection at the strategic core; poorer districts absorbed the rest.
    The costThe city must admit survival was courage and calculated sacrifice.

    What happened at White Mesa?

    The lieBoy Thunder’s finest hour.
    The truthThe ordered overload killed civilians, prisoners, and his own cadets — then Bloodline edited his memory and the footage.
    The costTomas must own the act without carrying it alone.

    Who launched the Fourfold Fall?

    The lieEach corporation blames the others.
    The truthThe shared deterrent self-escalated after someone seeded authentic launch authority across all three networks.
    The costNo single enemy can absorb the blame.

    Is Vin alive?

    The lieDead in gang territory, 2079.
    The truthHis uncle held him — Bright House, then the program — for five years.
    The costNicole’s public sainthood stands on a managed lie.

    Every answer changes a relationship or forces a decision. Answers are never trivia.
